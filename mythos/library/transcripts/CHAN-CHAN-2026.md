# ARCH-001 — Chan Chan Funerary Platform

**Collection:** Chimú / Mortuary archaeology  
**Evidence state:** Corroborated  
**Date:** approximately 600 years ago  

## Abstract
Archaeologists excavated a funerary platform at Chan Chan, the capital of the Chimú Kingdom on Peru's northern coast. The structure contained the remains of 38 individuals and associated objects including shell, copper, gold, weapons and other burial goods.

## Claim decomposition
**C1 — A substantial Chimú funerary complex was preserved at the site.** Corroborated.

**C2 — The individuals formed a single socially coherent group.** Undetermined pending osteological, isotopic and contextual analysis.

**C3 — The burial goods can be treated as direct indicators of rank.** Plausible, but not demonstrated for every object.

## Evidence boundary
The excavation provides physical context. It does not automatically establish the identities, kinship relations, causes of death or exact political status of every buried individual.

## Why it matters
Chan Chan is often represented through monumental adobe architecture alone. This record adds mortuary organization, material wealth and social structure to the same civilization's evidence graph.
