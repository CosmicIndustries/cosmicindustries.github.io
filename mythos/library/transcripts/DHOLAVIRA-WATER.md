# ARCH-007 — Dholavira Water Management

**Collection:** Harappan civilization / Hydraulic engineering  
**Evidence state:** Corroborated  
**Date:** approximately 3000–1500 BCE  

## Abstract
Dholavira, a major Harappan settlement on the arid island of Khadir, contains substantial water-management infrastructure. UNESCO identifies reservoirs and related systems as central to the settlement's adaptation to scarce water.

## Claim decomposition
**C1 — Dholavira used engineered water storage.** Corroborated.

**C2 — The entire hydraulic system was centrally controlled by a single authority.** Undetermined.

**C3 — Similar water systems elsewhere prove cultural transmission from Dholavira.** Contradicted as a general inference; similarity can arise through convergent adaptation.

## Evidence boundary
The physical reservoirs, settlement plan and environmental setting establish engineering practice. They do not by themselves reveal the complete administrative structure or every seasonal operating procedure.

## Why it matters
Environmental engineering belongs in the same historical graph as politics, economy and belief. Scarcity can produce recurring technological solutions without requiring common cultural ancestry.
