# ATLANTIS-PLATO — Digital Transcript

**Collection:** Constructed Ancient Histories  
**Era:** Ancient Greece · 4th century BCE  
**Evidence state:** Undetermined for historical Atlantis; plausible for deliberate literary construction  
**Record type:** Text / provenance audit

## Abstract
Plato's *Timaeus* and *Critias* contain a detailed account of Atlantis framed as ancient history transmitted through Solon and Egyptian priests. No independent archaeological or textual chain has established the described empire.

## Claim decomposition
### C1 — Atlantis existed substantially as described
**Status:** Undetermined  
The surviving Platonic text is evidence that Plato presented the story; it is not independent evidence that the described polity existed.

### C2 — The story was deliberately constructed for philosophical purposes
**Status:** Plausible / strongly supported by literary analysis  
The narrative supplies a remote chronology, elaborate provenance, and an idealized ancient Athens useful to the philosophical argument.

## Provenance chain
Plato → literary dialogue → reported Solon tradition → reported Egyptian priestly record.

**Audit finding:** every link after Plato is internal to the narrative. No surviving external archive closes the chain.

## Counterevidence
- No securely identified Atlantean inscription or administrative record.
- No independently attested historical chronology matching the account.
- No archaeological assemblage securely attributable to Plato's Atlantis.

## Interpretation boundary
“Plato invented a philosophical historical model” is a claim about literary construction.  
“Plato knowingly deceived his audience for amusement” is a stronger claim requiring evidence of private intention.
