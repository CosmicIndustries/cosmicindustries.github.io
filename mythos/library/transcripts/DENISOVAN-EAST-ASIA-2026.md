# DEEP-018 — Denisovan East Asian Archaeology

**Collection:** Deep time / Paleoanthropology  
**Evidence state:** Corroborated for Denisovan occupation; attribution of every artifact remains bounded by context  
**Date:** Pleistocene; reported occupation extends at least ~30,000 years and may be substantially longer  

## Abstract
New research from southwestern China combines fossils and a large artifact assemblage to expand the Denisovan archaeological record beyond Denisova Cave. Evidence includes bone tools and hunting-related remains.

## Claim decomposition
**C1 — Denisovans occupied the southwestern Chinese site.** Corroborated by the combined fossil/protein/archaeological evidence reported by researchers.

**C2 — Denisovans were capable hunters and toolmakers.** Supported by the associated artifact and faunal evidence.

**C3 — Every artifact at the site was made by Denisovans.** Not established solely by association; attribution should remain item-specific.

## Memory boundary
This record demonstrates that the Denisovan world was geographically and behaviorally broader than the original Siberian reference site. It does not provide evidence for a surviving Denisovan oral tradition.

## Why it matters
Deep-time history is especially vulnerable to false gaps. New fossil and protein evidence can change the map without requiring a new species or civilization narrative to be invented around it.
