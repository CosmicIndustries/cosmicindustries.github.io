Evidence without contempt

MYTHOS documents what people believed, then evaluates individual empirical propositions independently. A tradition can be culturally meaningful, historically attested, partially supported, or empirically contradicted without reducing the people who held it to a caricature.

Important: “oldest” is a claim, not a starting assumption. Egypt is extraordinarily ancient, but the current archaeological record does not justify simply calling Egypt the oldest civilization or Egyptian writing the first writing.

Egypt: starting point, not automatic winner

Ancient Egypt is one of humanity's earliest literate state societies. Egyptian hieroglyphic writing appears by the late 4th millennium BCE. Mesopotamian proto-cuneiform is similarly early, so the atlas treats both as part of the earliest documented writing horizon rather than declaring one civilization the universal “first.”

Predynastic Egypt — symbolic systems develop before full writing.
c. 3400–3200 BCE — early Egyptian writing emerges in the archaeological record.
Early Dynastic period — writing becomes increasingly integrated with administration, kingship and ritual.
Later periods — Egyptian religious concepts change over time; “Egyptian mythology” is therefore treated as a moving corpus, not a single fixed doctrine.

Recovered & declassified evidence

MYTHOS will maintain a separate archival layer for material that became public through declassification, archival release, litigation, FOIA, or newly digitized collections.

Rule: declassified does not mean true; classified does not mean extraordinary; unexplained does not mean supernatural. The underlying document and provenance are the evidence.
