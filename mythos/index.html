Evidence without contempt

MYTHOS documents what people believed, then evaluates individual empirical propositions independently. A tradition can be culturally meaningful, historically attested, partially supported, or empirically contradicted without reducing the people who held it to a caricature.

Library: The growing corpus now has a human-readable digital transcript layer plus an associative-memory prototype. The transcript/source chain remains authoritative; the vector layer is an index for finding relationships.

Important: “oldest” is a claim, not a starting assumption. Egypt is extraordinarily ancient, but the current archaeological record does not justify simply calling Egypt the oldest civilization or Egyptian writing the first writing.

Cross-correlation engine

MYTHOS does not assume that similar stories are the same story. It compares motifs, event order, relationships, geography, chronology, linguistic evidence and transmission pathways. Similarity is a lead for investigation, not proof of common origin.

Methodological warning: Campbell's “hero's journey” is influential but treating it as proof of a single universal story is too strong. Trickster & fabrication audit

Not every trickster is a fraud. MYTHOS separates the mythic trickster archetype—a culturally acknowledged figure such as Loki, Anansi, Coyote or Hermes—from the historical fabrication pattern: invented antiquity, forged provenance, false attribution, or evidence staged to acquire authority.

Plato case: the atlas does not label Plato a “liar.” The useful question is narrower: does the Atlantis narrative function as independent history, or as constructed pseudo-history serving a philosophical purpose? Several modern scholarly treatments argue for the latter, while the precise literary status remains debated. citeturn705217search0turn705217search1turn705217search5

Audit rule: first establish that the document/object/story exists; then establish provenance; then establish what the source actually claims; only then evaluate the extraordinary interpretation.

Recovered knowledge & technology

Some historical claims look extraordinary because the surviving evidence is incomplete. MYTHOS tracks a separate class for real artifacts, techniques, materials and observations whose capabilities were misunderstood, forgotten, rediscovered or reconstructed experimentally.

Guardrail: recovering an ancient capability does not validate unrelated “advanced ancient civilization” claims. The artifact, technique and exact claimed capability must each be demonstrated independently.

Deep time & ancestral memory

Before written history, the atlas tracks what can be reconstructed from fossils, sediments, genetics, artifacts, paleoclimate and later oral traditions. A proposed “memory” is never promoted to history merely because a story resembles an ancient environmental event.

Memory test: identify the event → date the event → date the oldest attestation of the story → establish a plausible cultural transmission chain → compare the story against independent geological/archaeological evidence.

Temporal architecture

MYTHOS is being built from the two ends inward: establish the ancient evidence base and the present-day record first, then work through the intervening historical layers. This prevents modern folklore from being projected backward and prevents ancient traditions from being treated as timeless.

ANCIENT · deep time → c. 500 CE
Prehistory, first cities, writing, early states, ancient religions, oral traditions, archaeology, paleoclimate and early science.
PRESENT · c. 1900 CE → now
Modern folklore, religions, paranormal claims, UAP, cryptids, conspiracy traditions, scientific anomalies, intelligence records, FOIA and declassification.
MIDDLE · bridge corpus
Once the endpoints are mapped, reconstruct transmission, transformation and disappearance across Late Antiquity, medieval, early-modern and industrial periods.

Egypt: starting point, not automatic winner

Ancient Egypt is one of humanity's earliest literate state societies. Egyptian hieroglyphic writing appears by the late 4th millennium BCE. Mesopotamian proto-cuneiform is similarly early, so the atlas treats both as part of the earliest documented writing horizon rather than declaring one civilization the universal “first.”

Predynastic Egypt — symbolic systems develop before full writing.
c. 3400–3200 BCE — early Egyptian writing emerges in the archaeological record.
Early Dynastic period — writing becomes increasingly integrated with administration, kingship and ritual.
Later periods — Egyptian religious concepts change over time; “Egyptian mythology” is therefore treated as a moving corpus, not a single fixed doctrine.

Recovered & declassified evidence

MYTHOS will maintain a separate archival layer for material that became public through declassification, archival release, litigation, FOIA, or newly digitized collections.

Rule: declassified does not mean true; classified does not mean extraordinary; unexplained does not mean supernatural. The underlying document and provenance are the evidence.
