# FORT KNOWLEDGE — EPISTEMIC LITMUS & ADAPTIVE LEARNING PROTOCOL

Version: 0.1
Established: 2026-09-21

## Purpose

Determine whether a reader has enough understanding to continue at the current conceptual pace without turning the archive into a knowledge gate.

The test is:
- diagnostic, not punitive
- continuous, not pass/fail
- evidence-centered, not credential-centered
- adaptive, not exclusionary
- independent of formal education
- designed to preserve curiosity and momentum

A reader may access the corpus regardless of test state.

## Core rule

**Access is open. Claims are not automatically accepted.**

A person can read anything.

A claim enters the evidentiary graph only when its support is sufficient for the claim being made.

Unsupported claims are labeled:
- UNCORROBORATED
- UNDER_EVIDENCED
- DISPUTED
- CONTRADICTED
- DISCONFIRMED
- UNKNOWN

"Insufficient evidence" is not automatically "false."

## 1. What the litmus measures

The minimum competencies are:

### A. Claim recognition

Can the reader distinguish:
- observation
- measurement
- interpretation
- hypothesis
- opinion
- prediction
- narrative
- moral judgment

### B. Evidence matching

Can the reader identify evidence appropriate to the claim?

Examples:
- historical claim -> primary records
- physical claim -> measurement/experiment
- statistical claim -> dataset + method
- causal claim -> design capable of supporting causality
- location claim -> reference frame + epoch
- identity claim -> provenance + corroboration

### C. Uncertainty handling

Can the reader distinguish:
- known
- estimated
- inferred
- plausible
- speculative
- unknown

### D. Source reasoning

Can the reader ask:
- Who produced this?
- When?
- Why?
- From what original evidence?
- Has it changed?
- Can it be independently checked?

### E. Correlation discipline

Can the reader avoid treating:
correlation -> causation

and instead ask what mechanism or experimental design would justify a causal conclusion?

### F. Revision

Can the reader update a belief when stronger evidence appears without treating revision as failure?

## 2. The litmus format

The test uses tiny real-world prompts rather than a school-style exam.

Each prompt presents:

1. a claim
2. a small evidence bundle
3. a conflicting or limiting detail
4. one decision point

The reader chooses or writes:

- What is actually established?
- What is inferred?
- What remains unknown?
- What evidence would change the conclusion?

## 3. Three-speed adaptive loop

### GREEN — ready pace

The reader correctly separates evidence from interpretation.

Continue normally.

### AMBER — friction detected

The reader understands the topic but is making one or more repeatable reasoning errors.

Do not stop access.

Insert a micro-lesson, example, diagram, or extra source before the next difficult concept.

### BLUE — terminology friction

The reasoning is sound but vocabulary, notation, units, language, or domain conventions are causing confusion.

Translate/reframe rather than slowing the scientific reasoning.

### RED — evidence failure

The reader repeatedly treats unsupported claims as established evidence.

Do not gate access.

Instead:
- show the missing evidence
- identify what would qualify
- provide a worked example
- re-test with a new domain

The system continues only when the reasoning catches up.

## 4. No permanent score

Do not assign a fixed intelligence or competence score.

The state is local and temporary:

concept X:
    READY

concept Y:
    NEEDS_CONTEXT

concept Z:
    NEEDS_EVIDENCE_TRAINING

A person can be advanced in orbital mechanics and novice in archival chemistry without contradiction.

## 5. Minimal test

A five-minute litmus can use four prompts.

### Test 1 — Observation vs interpretation

"Instrument A recorded X."

Question:
What is directly established?

### Test 2 — Evidence strength

"Source B claims Y, but the raw dataset is unavailable."

Question:
How should Y be represented?

Correct direction:
claim preserved; evidentiary status remains limited.

### Test 3 — Correlation

"Variable A increased whenever variable B increased."

Question:
What additional evidence supports causality?

### Test 4 — Revision

"A later controlled study contradicts an earlier result."

Question:
What should happen to the earlier claim?

Correct direction:
retain the historical claim, attach the contradiction, update evidentiary status.

## 6. Claim admission rule

A claim should enter the main evidentiary layer only with:

CLAIM
+
PROVENANCE
+
EVIDENCE
+
SCOPE
+
UNCERTAINTY
+
COUNTEREVIDENCE STATUS

If one is missing, the claim may still remain publicly accessible, but its evidentiary state must say what is missing.

## 7. Evidence tiers

### E0 — assertion

Someone says it.

### E1 — sourced assertion

Someone says it and identifies a source.

### E2 — primary record

The original record or measurement is available.

### E3 — independently corroborated

Separate evidence agrees.

### E4 — reproduced

Independent investigators reproduce the result.

### E5 — convergent evidence

Multiple independent methods agree with compatible uncertainty.

These are not universal truth scores.

A historical event may never reach E4 because reproduction is impossible.

## 8. Claim complexity matching

The evidence threshold must scale with the claim.

Example:

Claim:
"Paper X says Y."

E1 can establish that.

Claim:
"Y happened."

Requires stronger historical evidence.

Claim:
"Y caused Z."

Requires causal evidence.

Claim:
"Y always causes Z."

Requires broad evidence and scope.

Claim:
"Y will happen next year."

Requires a predictive model plus uncertainty.

## 9. Learning pace

The system should maintain a conceptual frontier:

KNOWN
───────┤──── CURRENT LESSON ├─────── UNKNOWN

Each reader has a moving frontier.

When friction appears:
1. do not gate
2. identify the missing concept
3. supply the smallest useful explanation
4. test once
5. resume the original path

This prevents unnecessary remedial detours.

## 10. Voluntary depth

Every concept should support:

READ
    ↓
UNDERSTAND
    ↓
VERIFY
    ↓
DEEP DIVE

A reader who wants primary papers, raw data, mathematical derivations, or opposing interpretations can continue deeper at any time.

The adaptive layer controls pacing, not access.

## 11. No authority substitution

The litmus must never become:
"Do you agree with the Atlas?"

It asks:
"Can you identify what the evidence establishes and what it does not?"

A reader can legitimately disagree with the Atlas interpretation if they can provide better evidence or a stronger argument.

## 12. Claim disposition

When a claim lacks adequate support:

DO NOT:
- erase it
- call the person stupid
- infer bad intent
- hide it

DO:
- preserve it
- label the evidentiary state
- show missing support
- link counterevidence
- provide a route to verification

This keeps the archive open without making it epistemically indiscriminate.

## 13. Narrative and mnemonic handling

A memorable story can be retained as a mnemonic even when its factual status is uncertain.

Display:

STORY
    ↓
WHAT IT HELPS REMEMBER
    ↓
EVIDENCE
    ↓
WHAT IS ACTUALLY ESTABLISHED

The story's mnemonic value and factual reliability are separate properties.

## 14. Automated diagnostics

For each response or user-submitted claim, the system may detect:

- unsupported assertion
- missing source
- source mismatch
- overgeneralization
- correlation/causation error
- temporal mismatch
- reference-frame mismatch
- unit mismatch
- identity conflation
- appeal to authority
- circular reasoning
- cherry-picking
- survivorship bias
- narrative/evidence conflation

The system should explain the smallest correction needed.

## 15. Re-entry

A reader who previously struggled should encounter the concept again naturally.

No permanent stigma.

No "failed user" state.

Knowledge can decay and return.

The system should permit:
RETRY -> MICRO-LESSON -> NEW EXAMPLE -> CONTINUE

## 16. The ultimate test

A reader is ready to proceed when they can reliably answer four questions:

1. What do we know?
2. How do we know it?
3. What do we not know?
4. What would change our mind?

That is sufficient for continued exploration.

## 17. Core principle

**The archive never asks permission to learn. It only asks that claims earn evidentiary status.**

A person may explore freely.

Evidence determines what gets promoted from:
story -> claim -> supported claim -> established result.
